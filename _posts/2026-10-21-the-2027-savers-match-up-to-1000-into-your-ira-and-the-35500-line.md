![The 2027 Saver's Match: Up to $1,000 Into Your IRA and the $35,500 Line](/assets/pin-images/2026-10-21-the-2027-savers-match-up-to-1000-into-your-ira-and-the-35500-line.png)

Starting with retirement contributions made for 2027, the federal government will put money into some savers' accounts directly. A worker who contributes $2,000 to a 401(k) or an IRA for 2027, and whose income is under the program's limits, can have up to $1,000 more deposited by the Treasury after the 2027 return is filed. Owing no income tax doesn't matter. The program is the Saver's Match. Congress created it in the SECURE 2.0 Act at the end of 2022, and for contributions to retirement plans and IRAs it replaces the Saver's Credit.

On August 7, the Treasury Department and the IRS released Notice 2026-48, a detailed description of how the match is expected to work: who qualifies, how income is measured, how retirement withdrawals cut the match, which accounts can receive it, and how it would be claimed on a new Form 8880-A. Comments were due October 5. On October 9, the IRS canceled the public hearing on a related proposal about which noncitizens can receive refundable federal tax payments, because nobody asked to testify. As of October 17, no proposed regulations on the match itself had appeared in the Federal Register.

For people who keep most of their money in a bank, two pieces of the design matter more than the headline number. Interest from savings accounts, CDs and Treasury bills is part of the income that sets the size of the match. And withdrawals from IRAs and workplace plans in 2025 and 2026 reduce the contributions a 2027 match is based on, before the match is even figured.

The rules below come from [Notice 2026-48](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-26-48.pdf); the IRS news release [IR-2026-89](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/treasury-irs-begin-implementing-executive-order-14403-by-announcing-intent-to-issue-proposed-regulations-on-savers-match-which-will-benefit-millions-of-low-and-moderate-income-taxpayers); [Executive Order 14403](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/05/05/2026-08908/promoting-retirement-savings-access-for-american-workers-by-establishing-trumpiragov), published at 91 FR 24329; the [proposed regulations](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/08/20/2026-16985/application-of-the-personal-responsibility-and-work-opportunity-reconciliation-act-of-1996-to-the) on refundable credits and the 1996 welfare law, published at 91 FR 53812, and the [hearing cancellation](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/10/09/2026-20790/application-of-the-personal-responsibility-and-work-opportunity-reconciliation-act-of-1996-to-the) at 91 FR 64631; the 2026 retirement limits in [IR-2025-111](https://www.irs.gov/newsroom/401k-limit-increases-to-24500-for-2026-ira-limit-increases-to-7500) and [Notice 2025-67](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-drop/n-25-67.pdf); the IRS [Saver's Credit page](https://www.irs.gov/retirement-plans/plan-participant-employee/retirement-savings-contributions-credit-savers-credit); the 2025 [Form 1040](https://www.irs.gov/pub/irs-pdf/f1040.pdf); IRS [Topic No. 557](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc557) and [Topic No. 403](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc403); and the [FDIC](https://www.fdic.gov/resources/deposit-insurance)'s [guide to certain retirement accounts](https://www.fdic.gov/financial-institution-employees-guide-deposit-insurance/certain-retirement-accounts). The notice describes rules the Treasury and IRS "intend to propose regulations consistent with," so details can still change.

## What the match is

The notice puts the core rule in one sentence: Saver's Match contributions "are equal to up to 50 percent of $2,000 of qualified retirement savings contributions made by an eligible individual to an employer-sponsored retirement plan or an individual retirement account or annuity." Fifty percent of $2,000 is $1,000, which is the most one person can receive for a year. The $2,000 base is fixed. The notice says the contributions are counted "up to $2,000, not adjusted for cost-of-living increases."

The big change from the Saver's Credit is where the money goes. In the notice's words, Saver's Match contributions "are amounts that are generally paid directly to an eligible individual's applicable retirement savings vehicle, even if the individual has no income tax liability. In contrast, the Saver's Credit is a nonrefundable tax credit that cannot exceed the amount of an individual's tax liability." A low-income worker whose standard deduction already wipes out the tax bill gets nothing from the Saver's Credit. The match doesn't depend on tax owed.

The timing follows the tax return. The statute says the match is paid into the saver's account "as soon as practicable after" the return claiming it is filed. The IRS news release says the match "will be paid for eligible taxpayers starting in 2028, based on contributions made for the 2027 tax year."

Four other points from the notice:

- **It doesn't count toward contribution limits.** A match deposited in an IRA or a 401(k) isn't counted against the annual IRA or elective-deferral limits.
- **It doesn't feed next year's match.** Qualified retirement savings contributions "do not include any amount attributable to a payment of Saver's Match contributions."
- **Refund offsets can't reduce it.** The payment "is not subject to reduction or offset under section 6402(c), (d), (e), or (f) or any similar authority permitting offset and is not reduced or offset by other assessed federal taxes that would otherwise be subject to levy or collection." Those subsections of [section 6402](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/26/6402) cover offsets for past-due support, debts owed to federal agencies, past-due state income tax and unemployment compensation debts.
- **ABLE accounts stay with the credit.** Contributions to an ABLE account don't count toward the match. The Saver's Credit continues after 2026 for ABLE contributions, under a change in the 2025 tax law.

## Who can get it

The eligibility test is short. An individual must be at least 18 by the end of the year. Three groups are excluded: students, people claimed as a dependent on someone else's return, and nonresident aliens who haven't elected to be treated as U.S. residents. A student, under the definition the notice cites, is someone who during each of five months of the year was enrolled full-time at a school or took a full-time on-farm training course. A hypothetical 19-year-old with a part-time job who is enrolled full-time for five months of the year is out, even if he or she files a return.

There's a second screen still being written. A footnote in the notice says the Justice Department's Office of Legal Counsel concluded in a November 19, 2025 opinion that Saver's Match contributions are "federal public benefits" under the Personal Responsibility and Work Opportunity Reconciliation Act of 1996, and that the 1996 law "generally prohibits aliens who are not qualified aliens from being eligible to receive a federal public benefit." The notice adds: "The Treasury Department and the IRS anticipate addressing the applicability of PRWORA to Saver's Match contributions in forthcoming proposed regulations." The August 20 proposed regulations, which apply the 1996 law to the refunded portion of credits such as the earned income credit, leave the match for later: the agencies "intend to promulgate proposed regulations regarding the Saver's Match separately."

For married couples filing jointly, eligibility is personal but income is shared. The notice says eligibility "is determined independently for the individual and the individual's spouse," so each spouse can qualify for up to $1,000 based on his or her own contributions to his or her own plan or IRA. The income test uses "the combined MAGI of both spouses."

## The 2027 income lines

The match starts at 50% and phases down to zero, one percentage point at a time, over a range of modified adjusted gross income (MAGI). That is different from the Saver's Credit, which has three rates: 50%, 20% and 10%. The notice gives the 2027 figures by filing status:

| Filing status | Phaseout starts at MAGI of | Phaseout range | No match at MAGI of |
|---|---|---|---|
| Single | $20,500 | $15,000 | $35,500 |
| Married filing separately | $20,500 | $15,000 | $35,500 |
| Head of household | $30,750 | $22,500 | $53,250 |
| Married filing jointly | $41,000 | $30,000 | $71,000 |
| Surviving spouse | $41,000 | $30,000 | $71,000 |

Because the reduction is rounded down to a whole percentage point, the full 50% rate actually holds until MAGI reaches one full step above the starting point: $20,800 for single filers and married people filing separately, $31,200 for heads of household and $41,600 for joint filers and surviving spouses. The last column is the cutoff. The notice says a saver "is not entitled to claim a Saver's Match contribution for the 2027 taxable year if the individual's MAGI equals or exceeds" that amount.

These numbers are written into the statute for 2027. Inflation adjustments begin with tax years starting in a calendar year after 2027. They move the start and end of each phaseout range but not its width. The notice says the applicable dollar amount for each filing status "is adjusted for inflation for taxable years beginning after 2027, but the phaseout ranges are not adjusted for inflation." The base figure of $41,000 is adjusted and rounded to the nearest $1,000, and the other filing statuses are derived from it.

The 2027 cutoffs sit well below the limits for the credit being replaced. For 2026, IR-2025-111 lists the Saver's Credit income limit as "$80,500 for married couples filing jointly," "$60,375 for heads of household," and "$40,250 for singles and married individuals filing separately." A hypothetical single filer with $38,000 of AGI could still claim a 10% Saver's Credit for 2026 if he or she owed enough tax to use it. At the same income in 2027, with nothing to add back, the match is zero.

## What counts as income

MAGI for the match isn't the same as the income figure on the front of the return. The notice lists four pieces that are added together:

1. Adjusted gross income. The notice's footnote says this "appears on line 11a on the 2025 Form 1040."
2. Pre-tax elective deferrals and other salary reduction contributions to retirement plans, the amount that generally shows up in box 12 with code D on a W-2 for 401(k) deferrals.
3. Deductible contributions to traditional IRAs, the IRA deduction on Schedule 1.
4. Foreign earned income and certain U.S. territory income excluded under sections 911, 931 and 933.

Savings interest enters through the first item. On the 2025 Form 1040, taxable interest is line 2b. It's added into total income on line 9, and line 11a, "your adjusted gross income," is figured from there. IRS [Topic No. 403](https://www.irs.gov/taxtopics/tc403) lists "Interest on bank accounts, money market accounts, certificates of deposit" as taxable interest, and it counts credit union share dividends as interest too. Treasury bill interest "is subject to federal income tax but is exempt from all state and local income taxes." All of it raises MAGI for the match. Tax-exempt interest goes on line 2a, which isn't part of the line 9 total, and it isn't one of the notice's four add-backs.

Items 2 and 3 change a familiar piece of planning. Under the old credit, a deductible IRA contribution lowered the AGI that set the credit rate. The IRS Saver's Credit page gives an example of a married worker who "earned $41,000 in 2021" and contributed $2,000 to an IRA: "After deducting her IRA contribution, the adjusted gross income shown on her joint return is $39,000," which put her in the 50% tier. For the match, the deduction and pre-tax 401(k) deferrals are added back. Putting more into a traditional IRA or a pre-tax 401(k) doesn't lower the income used for the match.

## How the percentage is figured

The match rate starts at 50% and loses percentage points as MAGI rises above the applicable dollar amount. The notice's formula is: percentage point reduction equals 50 percentage points times the ratio of (MAGI minus the applicable dollar amount) to the phaseout range. The result is "rounded down to the next lowest whole percentage point and subtracted from 50%."

The notice's first example uses a single filer who put $1,500 into a traditional IRA in 2027 with MAGI of $30,000:

1. 50 × ($30,000 − $20,500) ÷ $15,000 = 31.6667 percentage points
2. Rounded down to 31
3. 50% − 31 = 19%
4. $1,500 × 19% = $285

Because the phaseout range is split into 50 steps, each filing status has a fixed income width per percentage point: $300 for single filers ($15,000 ÷ 50), $450 for heads of household ($22,500 ÷ 50) and $600 for joint filers ($30,000 ÷ 50). On a full $2,000 contribution, each point is worth $20. Here is the match on $2,000 at selected MAGI levels, figured with the notice's formula:

| Match rate | Single MAGI | Head of household MAGI | Joint MAGI | Match on $2,000 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 50% | under $20,800 | under $31,200 | under $41,600 | $1,000 |
| 40% | $23,500 | $35,250 | $47,000 | $800 |
| 30% | $26,500 | $39,750 | $53,000 | $600 |
| 20% | $29,500 | $44,250 | $59,000 | $400 |
| 10% | $32,500 | $48,750 | $65,000 | $200 |
| 0% | $35,500 | $53,250 | $71,000 | $0 |

Married people filing separately use the single column, and surviving spouses use the joint column.

Rounding down favors the saver inside each step. A single filer at $23,799 of MAGI has a reduction of about 10.997 points, which rounds down to 10, so the rate is still 40%. One more dollar of income moves it to 39%.

Near the top of the range the match gets small. A single filer at $34,300 of MAGI is at 4%, or $80 on $2,000. For a match "of greater than zero but less than $100," the saver may choose to take it as a refundable income tax credit on the return instead of having it deposited in an account. The notice says the $100 test "is applied on an individual basis," so each spouse on a joint return decides separately.

## Worked example: what savings interest costs

The following examples are hypothetical, and the math uses the notice's formula. Results are rounded to whole dollars.

**A single filer.** She earns $25,000 in wages in 2027 with no pre-tax 401(k) deferrals, earns $800 of interest on an emergency fund in a high-yield savings account (about what $20,000 would earn at a hypothetical 4% APY), and contributes $2,000 to a traditional IRA that she deducts.

- AGI: $25,000 + $800 − $2,000 = $23,800
- MAGI: $23,800 + $2,000 deductible IRA contribution added back = $25,800
- Reduction: 50 × ($25,800 − $20,500) ÷ $15,000 = 17.67, rounded down to 17
- Rate: 33%, so the match is $660

Without the $800 of interest, her MAGI would be $25,000, the reduction exactly 15 points, the rate 35% and the match $700. The interest cost her $40 of match, which is the $20-per-$300 rule at work: $800 crosses two full $300 steps and part of a third.

**A married couple filing jointly.** Their 2027 salaries total $60,000 before deferrals. One spouse defers $2,000 pre-tax into a 401(k). The other contributes $1,000 to a Roth IRA. Their savings account and CDs pay $1,200 of interest.

- AGI: $58,000 of taxable wages + $1,200 = $59,200
- MAGI: $59,200 + $2,000 pre-tax deferral = $61,200
- Reduction: 50 × ($61,200 − $41,000) ÷ $30,000 = 33.67, rounded down to 33
- Rate: 17%. The 401(k) saver's match is $340 and the Roth IRA saver's match is $170, $510 in total.

Without the interest, joint MAGI is $60,000, the rate 19%, and the matches $380 and $190, $570 in total. The $1,200 of interest cost them $60. The Roth contribution counts toward the match even though, as explained below, the match itself can't be paid straight into a Roth IRA.

Before tax on the interest, the interest is far larger than the lost match in both examples: the single filer earns $800 and loses $40. A larger amount, such as a year's interest on a sizable CD or Treasury bill position, can push a saver down several steps or, near the top of the range, past the cutoff.

## Contributions that count

The notice lists four kinds of qualified retirement savings contributions:

1. contributions to a traditional or Roth IRA
2. elective deferrals, such as deferrals to a 401(k) (including a SIMPLE 401(k)), 403(b), governmental 457(b), SIMPLE IRA plan or SEP arrangement
3. voluntary after-tax employee contributions to a qualified retirement plan
4. contributions to a section 501(c)(18) plan

Deferrals and after-tax contributions to governmental and church plans count, and so do contributions to the federal Thrift Savings Plan. Rollovers and trustee-to-trustee transfers don't count, and neither does a prior Saver's Match deposit.

Timing follows the usual IRA rule. Contributions generally must be made by the end of the year. IRA contributions can be made "as late as the tax filing deadline (without extensions) for the taxable year as long as the contributions are designated as being made for the prior taxable year." A tax refund can be the contribution, too. The notice says a saver "entitled to receive a tax refund for a taxable year can make qualified retirement savings contributions by requesting on Form 8888 that the individual's tax refund be contributed to an IRA." The IRS has to make that refund contribution by the filing deadline for it to count for that year. If it's made later, it counts for the year it's made.

One footnote matters for anyone funding an IRA early in 2027. An IRA contribution made after December 31, 2026 and by the due date of the 2026 return (without extensions) can be designated as a 2026 contribution, which goes toward a Saver's Credit on the 2026 return, or as a 2027 contribution, which goes toward the Saver's Match. The same dollars can't do both.

## Withdrawals that shrink the match

Withdrawals count against the contribution base. It is reduced "by the aggregate distributions received by the individual during a specified testing period" from IRAs and plans of the kinds that take qualified contributions. For a 2027 match, the testing period covers:

- the two preceding tax years, 2025 and 2026
- 2027 itself
- the period after 2027 and before the due date of the 2027 return, including extensions

On a joint return, a spouse's distributions count against the other spouse too, if the couple files jointly both for the year of the distribution and for the year the match is claimed.

The notice's own example: a saver contributes $2,000 to a 401(k) during 2027 but took a $500 IRA withdrawal in 2027 and a $900 IRA withdrawal in 2026, neither rolled over. The contributions counted for the match drop to "$600 ($2,000 - $500 - $900)." At a 50% rate that is a $300 match instead of $1,000.

The calendar makes this a live issue now. A withdrawal from an IRA or 401(k) taken this fall falls inside the 2026 part of the testing period for a 2027 match. So does one taken back in 2025. Take a hypothetical single saver with MAGI under $20,500 who withdraws $1,500 from an IRA in November 2026 and contributes $2,000 for 2027. The base drops to $500 and the match to $250.

Some amounts don't count as distributions for this purpose:

- plan loans treated as distributions, and corrective distributions of excess contributions or deferrals
- IRA contributions returned before the return's due date
- rollovers and trustee-to-trustee transfers, including any part of a distribution transferred or paid as a rollover contribution to an account or plan that accepts qualified contributions
- distributions from an IRA inherited from someone other than a spouse, which the notice says "are not from a retirement plan or IRA of a type to which qualified retirement savings contributions may be made"

The list of exclusions doesn't mention hardship withdrawals or emergency personal expense distributions. Under the general rule, a distribution that isn't excluded reduces the base. The notice doesn't say how an emergency distribution that is later repaid would be treated for this purpose.

## Where the match can be deposited

Apart from the under-$100 credit option described above, section 6433 limits where the Treasury can deposit the money. Eligible destinations are the non-Roth part of a 401(k), a 403(b) bought under a salary reduction agreement, or a governmental 457(b) plan, and "an individual retirement plan that is not a Roth IRA." The account has to be for the saver's benefit, accept Saver's Match contributions, and be designated by the saver. Government and church plans of those types qualify, and so does the Thrift Savings Plan.

Acceptance is voluntary. The notice says, "Neither retirement plans nor IRAs are required to accept Saver's Match contributions directly from the Treasury Department." A plan or IRA that does accept can set "reasonable conditions," such as accepting matches only for current employees or only for participants who already have balances. A plan has to be amended to accept matches directly from the Treasury, though the notice says the deadline for that kind of amendment is generally the end of the plan year in which the plan starts doing it. A plan that already accepts rollovers generally wouldn't need to be amended to take match money rolled over from a conduit IRA.

**Traditional IRAs.** The process "is still under development," according to Q&A E-2 of the notice. Under the approach it describes, an IRA provider would register with the Treasury and the IRS and receive an IRA tracking number. The saver would open the IRA first, then enter the provider's tracking number on Form 8880-A, and the match would be paid directly to that IRA. A saver whose existing IRA is at a provider that doesn't register could open an IRA at one that has, receive the match there, and then move it to the original IRA by trustee-to-trustee transfer.

**Roth IRAs.** The Treasury would not deposit the match into a Roth IRA. Instead it "would establish a conduit traditional IRA" for the saver, followed by "an immediate trustee-to-trustee transfer from the conduit IRA to the eligible individual's chosen Roth IRA." The notice says "This transfer would be a Roth IRA conversion that would be subject to federal income tax and reporting described in Treas. Reg. § 1.408A-4," and it would also be subject to the withholding rules in section 3405. A match directed to a traditional IRA or the pre-tax side of a plan doesn't go through that conversion step.

**Workplace plans.** Q&A E-3 describes three paths under consideration for plans: a Registration Path, where a registered plan receives the match by rollover from a conduit IRA; an Automatic Match Path, where plans send participant information so the Treasury can pay the plan directly; and a Rollover Path, where the saver gives the plan an IRS-issued Saver's Match Confirmation Number. The notice asked for comments on the best way to do this, so the process isn't settled.

**TrumpIRA.gov.** Executive Order 14403, signed April 30, directs the Treasury to launch TrumpIRA.gov by January 1, 2027. The site is to list financial institutions that offer IRAs, accept the match, and meet cost and quality criteria. The order's criteria include investment menus with target-date or balanced options, or "funds that are designed to protect principal on an ongoing basis," net expense ratios "limited to .15 percent," and IRAs that "do not impose minimum-contribution or balance requirements." The IRS release says more information for IRA providers that want to be listed "will be available later this year." The notice treats being listed on the site and being registered to receive matches as separate steps, and it asks for comments on whether IRA registration should be facilitated in connection with TrumpIRA.gov.

**Bank and credit union IRAs.** An IRA savings account or [IRA CD](/2026/09/12/ira-cds-insurance-rollovers-and-the-rmd-deadline/) at a bank is a traditional or Roth IRA held as a deposit. Under the notice's approach, having the Treasury pay the match straight into one depends on whether the bank or credit union registers as a provider, and the notice says registration details "will be provided in the future." An IRA at an institution that doesn't register could still receive the money afterward by trustee-to-trustee transfer, the route described above. The order's listing criteria are written around investment funds and portfolios, and the notice doesn't say how a deposit-only IRA would be treated for TrumpIRA.gov. If the match lands in an IRA deposit at an FDIC-insured bank, it's covered like the rest of that IRA: the FDIC guide says "Deposits in all Certain Retirement Accounts owned by the same depositor and held at the same IDI are added together and the total is insured for up to $250,000." IDI stands for insured depository institution.

## Claiming it on the return

The match is claimed, not automatic. Under Q&A E-1, a saver "would need to file an income tax return for that taxable year and claim the Saver's Match contribution on a separate Form 8880-A for that taxable year." Payment would follow "as soon as practicable" after the return is filed. The claim requires information about MAGI, filing status, qualified contributions and distributions during the testing period. The notice calls the form "a new (not yet published) Form 8880-A, Saver's Match for Qualified Retirement Savings Contributions (or a successor form)." Form 8880 is expected to stay in use for the Saver's Credit on ABLE contributions.

Because the match is claimed on a return, someone who doesn't normally file because income is below the filing threshold would have to file one to get it. The notice doesn't describe a separate application.

## Once the money is in the account

A match deposit is retirement money with retirement rules, and a few rules apply to it alone.

**Income tax comes later.** Saver's Match contributions "are not includible in gross income when they are contributed." Distributions attributable to them are taxed in the year they're taken, unless rolled over or transferred.

**Early withdrawals can trigger the 10% additional tax.** IRS Topic No. 557 says the tax applies "unless an exception applies," that "Generally, early distributions are those you receive from an IRA before reaching age 59½," and that the 10% additional tax "applies to the part of the distribution that you have to include in gross income." The notice points to the same rules for match money.

**The recovery tax.** Section 6433(f)(6) adds a Saver's Match recovery tax for one situation: an early distribution, subject to the 10% additional tax, is made from the account that received match deposits, and at the end of that year the aggregate match contributions exceed the account balance. The recovery tax equals that excess, reduced by the 10% additional tax on the distribution and by allocable investment losses. A saver can reduce it by putting back up to the amount of the early distribution by the return's due date, including extensions. The notice asks for comments on how to determine losses, so the mechanics aren't final.

Here is how the statute's arithmetic would work, in a hypothetical that assumes no exception to the 10% additional tax applies and nothing is put back. A 35-year-old receives a $1,000 match in a traditional IRA that already holds a deductible $2,000 contribution, and the account earns $100. A year later she withdraws the whole $3,100. The 10% additional tax is $310. Match deposits of $1,000 exceed the $0 year-end balance by $1,000, so the recovery tax is $1,000 − $310 = $690. Together the two equal the $1,000 match, on top of regular income tax on the $3,100. If she had withdrawn only $1,500 and left $1,600 in the account, the balance would still be above the $1,000 of match deposits. The recovery tax wouldn't apply, though the $150 additional tax would.

**Hardship withdrawals exclude match money in plans.** In a 401(k), 403(b) or governmental 457(b), the match can't be paid out as a hardship or unforeseeable-emergency distribution, though earnings on it can. The restriction doesn't apply to other in-service distributions, including [emergency personal expense distributions](/2026/10/10/the-1000-emergency-401k-withdrawal-vs-your-savings-account-the-rules/) and qualified disaster recovery distributions. Plans that allow hardship withdrawals will need to track match money separately.

## The 2026 Saver's Credit still applies

For contributions made for 2026, the Saver's Credit works as before. The IRS page says the credit is 50%, 20% or 10% of eligible contributions depending on AGI, and that "The maximum contribution amount that may qualify for the credit is $2,000 ($4,000 if married filing jointly), making the maximum credit $1,000 ($2,000 if married filing jointly)." Notice 2025-67 sets the 2026 tiers:

| 2026 credit rate | Married filing jointly (AGI) | Head of household (AGI) | All other filers (AGI) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 50% | up to $48,500 | up to $36,375 | up to $24,250 |
| 20% | $48,501 to $52,500 | $36,376 to $39,375 | $24,251 to $26,250 |
| 10% | $52,501 to $80,500 | $39,376 to $60,375 | $26,251 to $40,250 |

The credit is nonrefundable. It can reduce tax owed to zero but doesn't produce a refund beyond that, while the match doesn't depend on tax owed. The credit also still covers ABLE contributions, which the match doesn't.

## Common questions

**Do I have to owe income tax to get the match?**

No. The notice says the match is paid to the account "even if the individual has no income tax liability." You do have to file a return and Form 8880-A to claim it. If the match would be under $100, you can choose to take it as a refundable credit on the return instead.

**Does a deposit into my regular savings account count?**

No. Only contributions to IRAs, elective deferrals to workplace plans, voluntary after-tax plan contributions and 501(c)(18) plan contributions count. An IRA savings account or IRA CD at a bank is an IRA, so contributions to it count, but money in an ordinary savings account doesn't.

**Can the match go into my Roth IRA?**

Not directly. Under the approach in the notice, the Treasury would pay it into a conduit traditional IRA and move it to the Roth IRA by trustee-to-trustee transfer, which would be a taxable Roth conversion. Roth IRA contributions do count as qualified contributions for figuring the match.

**My spouse and I file jointly. Is the $1,000 limit per couple?**

Per person. Each spouse who is eligible and makes his or her own qualified contributions can receive up to $1,000 into his or her own account. The income test uses your combined MAGI.

Within the income limits, a $2,000 contribution for 2027 can bring in up to $1,000 more, with no tax liability needed to use it. The notice adds the fine print: the same interest that grows an emergency fund is counted in the income test, IRA and plan withdrawals going back to 2025 come off the contribution base, and match money taken out early can face the 10% additional tax and be pulled back through the recovery tax.
